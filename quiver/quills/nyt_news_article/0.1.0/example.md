The exercise, designated Resolute Shield 26, will involve more than 40 warships, three carrier strike groups and approximately 25,000 personnel from the United States, Japan, Australia, South Korea, the Philippines and seven other Indo-Pacific nations, according to Pentagon officials who spoke on the condition of background briefing rules.

Adm. James T. Hargrove, commander of the United States Pacific Fleet, described the exercise as "a clear demonstration of our collective commitment to a free and open Indo-Pacific" during a news conference aboard the U.S.S. Ronald Reagan at Fleet Activities Yokosuka.

The exercise, which is expected to last three weeks, will include live-fire drills, anti-submarine warfare operations, amphibious landing rehearsals and integrated air defense scenarios across a vast arc of the Pacific stretching from waters east of Okinawa to the Luzon Strait.

Secretary of Defense Maria Chen, who observed the opening ceremony via secure video link from the Pentagon, called the exercise "a powerful signal that the United States and our allies will continue to uphold the rules-based international order in the Indo-Pacific region."

The timing of the exercise has drawn attention from regional analysts, coming less than two weeks after satellite imagery revealed expanded construction at several contested features in the South China Sea. Beijing has not commented publicly on the exercise but the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a statement on Friday urging "relevant countries to refrain from actions that complicate the regional security environment."

Vice Adm. Kenji Yamashita of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force, who is serving as deputy exercise commander, told reporters that the exercise would test new interoperability protocols developed over the past 18 months. "This is not simply about demonstrating presence," Admiral Yamashita said. "It is about proving that our forces can fight together seamlessly."

Australia is contributing the guided-missile destroyer H.M.A.S. Hobart and a P-8A Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft, while South Korea has deployed the Aegis destroyer Sejong the Great and a submarine from its Chang Bogo-class fleet.

The exercise also includes a humanitarian assistance and disaster relief component, with participating nations rehearsing coordinated responses to a simulated typhoon striking a fictional Pacific island nation, an increasingly common scenario as climate change intensifies storm activity in the region.

Military analysts noted that the scale and scope of Resolute Shield 26 represented a significant increase from previous iterations. "This is the kind of exercise that takes years to plan," said Dr. Elena Vasquez, a senior fellow at the Center for Strategic and International Studies. "The fact that they were able to bring 12 nations together sends a very clear message."
