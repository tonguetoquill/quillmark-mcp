Multiple NATO warships participating in Exercise Northern Resolve have been diverted from planned training activities to track an unidentified submarine contact detected near the exercise area in the central Baltic Sea, according to three defense officials familiar with the situation.

The contact was first detected early Saturday by a Norwegian P-3C Orion maritime patrol aircraft conducting routine surveillance operations in support of the exercise, the officials said, speaking on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military operations.

NATO's Allied Maritime Command in Northwood, United Kingdom, confirmed that "maritime assets have been repositioned in response to an underwater contact of interest" but declined to provide additional details, citing operational security.

The detection comes during one of NATO's largest Baltic exercises this year, involving naval forces from 14 allied nations conducting anti-submarine warfare, mine countermeasures and amphibious operations across a broad swath of the Baltic Sea.

A senior European defense official told CNN that the contact was assessed as a diesel-electric submarine operating at shallow depth approximately 30 nautical miles from the main exercise area. The official said the submarine's acoustic signature did not match any known NATO or partner nation vessel scheduled to participate in the exercise.

Standing NATO Maritime Group 1, led by a German frigate, was redirected from planned exercises northeast of Gotland to assist in tracking the contact, according to a NATO military official. Additional anti-submarine warfare aircraft from Denmark and the United Kingdom were also dispatched to the area.

The incident has prompted heightened vigilance among Baltic NATO members, several of whom have increased maritime patrols in their exclusive economic zones in recent months following a series of incidents involving suspected sabotage of undersea infrastructure in the region.

A Pentagon spokesperson said the United States was "aware of the situation and in close coordination with our NATO allies" but referred further questions to NATO headquarters in Brussels.
